Why here

Four things we put in writing.

Home time in the lane, not in the pitch

Regional runs are built to get you home weekly, OTR on a set rotation. If a load would break that, it gets covered by someone else — you don't get asked to absorb it.

Pay you can predict

Detention, layover and stop pay are on the rate sheet, not discretionary. Settlements post on the same day each week, and you can see the math on every line.

Equipment that isn't a fight

Tractors on a replacement cycle, inspected on schedule, with a shop that treats a defect report as information rather than an accusation. If it's unsafe it doesn't roll.

A dispatcher, not a queue

You get a named planner and their direct line. No ticket system between you and the person who can actually move your appointment.

The process

Days, not weeks.

You will hear something from a person at every stage, including if the answer is no. Nobody gets left waiting on silence.

1
You apply Day 0

The form below, or a call to the recruiting line. A résumé helps but isn't required for driving and warehouse roles.

2
A real phone call Within 2 business days

Fifteen minutes covering the schedule, the pay and what you're actually looking for. Ask us anything — this is the stage to find a mismatch.

3
In person, on site Within a week

Meet the planner or shift lead you'd actually work with, walk the yard, and for driving roles, a road test in the equipment you'd be assigned.

4
Checks and paperwork 2–5 days

MVR, employment verification, DOT physical and drug screen where the role requires it. We tell you what we're checking before we check it.

5
Offer and start Same week

Written offer with the pay structure spelled out line by line. Orientation runs Mondays, and you're on a paid lane that week.

Benefits

  • Medical, dental and vision from day 31
  • 401(k) with company match
  • Paid time off that accrues from your first week
  • Per diem on OTR runs
  • Referral bonus for drivers you bring with you
  • Paid orientation — you're on the clock from hour one
